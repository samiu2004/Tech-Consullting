QuickBooks Online’s 2026 Price Change: Check Your Renewal Before Switching

Understand the announced US renewal change and compare your actual bill with the cost of staying or moving.

Before comparing a higher renewal with another app's introductory offer, check which price applies to your subscription. A regular renewal, a promotion and an accountant-managed account may not follow the same path.

Intuit's US announcement states that QuickBooks Online Essentials, Plus and Advanced prices change for renewals on or after 1 August 2026. This November guide is an explanation of that announced change, not a claim that it is breaking news today.

Confirm whether the announcement applies

Start with the product, region, edition and billing route shown on your account. Do not apply a US QuickBooks Online announcement to a similarly named product or another region without checking its own notice.

Read any promotion or account-specific message together with the announcement. Intuit describes conditions affecting when a recent subscriber's increase takes effect. The correct question is what applies to your subscription, not which screenshot someone else received.

Use a verified bill rather than a recycled price table

We have not reproduced the draft's current list-price amounts because the live pricing page was not successfully reverified during preparation. The effective-date announcement is supported; that does not automatically confirm every price repeated in secondary articles.

Use your renewal notice and the official pricing page to fill the worksheet below. Record the date and whether each amount is a regular rate, promotion or quote. If the numbers disagree, ask the provider to explain the applicable rate before deciding.

Calculate the difference on a consistent basis

For a hypothetical monthly increase from US$50 to US$60, the increase is US$10 per month and US$120 over twelve months at the new rate. The percentage increase is 20%. These figures illustrate the calculation and are not QuickBooks prices.

If the new rate begins partway through a planning period, calculate the affected months rather than applying a full-year increase to every month. Include other charges separately and do not compare a regular renewal with a competitor's short introductory discount as though both last indefinitely.

Check three decisions before migrating

First, determine whether the current edition is still needed. Ask what would be lost on a lower qualifying edition and whether the accountant or team relies on those functions. A downgrade that breaks a required workflow is not a saving.

Second, examine whether the higher ongoing cost is acceptable for the work the product supports. Keep your reasoning specific: reporting, existing integrations, staff familiarity and the records the business needs to maintain.

Third, compare a replacement's first-year total. Include setup, data conversion, parallel subscriptions, training and any limitation in transferred history. A lower subscription can still cost more during the transition.

Avoid a hurried cancellation

Preserve required records and check the export route before access changes. A software change should have acceptance criteria, not simply a target to avoid the next invoice. Use the migration checklist and cancellation guide for those steps.

The useful result is a documented choice to stay, change edition or investigate a replacement. Keep the notice, the calculation and the unresolved questions together. Review the applicable price again before taking action: a dated announcement explains a change, while your current account terms determine what you are being asked to pay.

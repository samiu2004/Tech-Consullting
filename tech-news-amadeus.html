Amadeus breach widens as Singapore Airlines reports 46,000 records affected

You did not lose money in this breach. That is exactly why the next email about your booking is dangerous.

By TechConsulting Editorial Published 4 November 2026 Sources checked 13 November 2026 2 min read

You did not lose money in this breach. That is exactly why the next email about your booking is dangerous.

Singapore Airlines said on 4 November that about 46,000 booking records may have been affected through reservation provider Amadeus. It joins Cathay Pacific, Japan Airlines, ANA, Malaysia Airlines, and Asiana in the same incident. The counts mix bookings and customers, so they are not additive. Amadeus says no payment data was taken and bookings were not modified. That is the good news.

The awkward part is what booking details alone can do. A name, a flight number, and a travel date are enough to write an email that looks exactly like it came from your airline. It can ask you to confirm a change, re-enter payment details, or click a link to view your booking. Nothing about it will look wrong, because the details in it are real.

Why it matters to you: this is a third-party breach, which means you did nothing wrong and could not have prevented it. Your data was held by a provider your airline uses, not by you. The risk now is not the breach itself. It is the convincing scam that follows it.

What to do now

  • Treat unexpected emails about your booking with care, even if they use your real flight details.
  • Do not click links in a booking email. Go to the airline website or app yourself and check there.
  • Never share payment details by email or through a link in a message.
  • If you flew with one of these airlines, stay alert for phishing that uses your trip details over the next few weeks.

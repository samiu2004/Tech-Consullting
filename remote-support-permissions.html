Remote Client Support: Consent, Access and Revocation Checklist

Manage client access through its full lifecycle, from approval to verified removal after the work ends.

A project can end while its remote access stays active. Plan how access ends before granting it, and keep a named owner responsible for the decision. Installing a trusted product does not, by itself, define who should be able to control a client's machine.

This checklist is for authorized client support. It is a practical policy template, not a claim that one configuration satisfies every contract or security requirement. Use the client's approved process and obtain the necessary review for sensitive work.

Choose an attended or unattended model deliberately

Attended support involves the client participating in the connection or approval process. Unattended access allows an authorized connection without someone approving each individual session in the same way. Check the exact product's behavior rather than assuming a label guarantees a particular consent screen.

Define the purpose, devices, named staff, permitted times and actions. A one-off troubleshooting task may not justify ongoing access. A maintenance engagement may need a different arrangement, but its scope and review date should still be explicit.

Check timeout and login-screen behavior

Splashtop's request-permission documentation describes configurable behavior. An unanswered request can be rejected or accepted depending on the selected option; login-screen and version details also matter. Do not describe a thirty-second timeout as a universal safeguard.

Test the actual approved setup: accepted request, declined request, no response, locked device and expired authorization. Record what the client sees and what the technician can do. Repeat relevant tests when configuration or product behavior changes.

Limit the account and the session

Use named accounts and appropriate authentication rather than a shared permanent credential. Grant only the permissions needed for the work. Discuss file transfer, clipboard, session recording and elevated access where those capabilities are relevant.

Ask which controls and execution history belong to the intended edition. A vendor offering audit features somewhere in its range does not establish that your proposed plan includes them. Also distinguish the number of technicians, registered devices and simultaneous connections when comparing costs.

Use a one-page access record

Field What the agency should record
Client and device Authorized scope without unnecessary sensitive detail
Purpose Task or maintenance agreement
Access model Attended or specifically approved unattended
Named users Who may connect and who approves changes
Permissions Allowed actions and prohibited actions
Review or expiry When continuing access is reconsidered
Session evidence Where appropriate logs are available
Closure Who removed access and how it was checked

Keep this record in an approved location and tell the client how to raise a concern or withdraw authorization. Do not publish client access records in a shared public document.

Review exceptions after a session

Record a problem if access behaved differently from the approval, a session failed or a user received permissions beyond the intended task. Assign the issue to an owner instead of treating it as routine troubleshooting.

Keep session evidence according to the agreement and applicable organizational requirements. Do not promise that a product log proves every action or that recording a session is appropriate without considering the client's requirements and consent.

Revoke and verify at closeout

Remove unneeded accounts, permissions, unattended credentials and device access according to the approved process. Cancel or transfer any tool ownership that depends on the departing worker. Confirm that the former access no longer works using an authorized check.

Tell the client the work and closeout are complete, and retain the appropriate confirmation. Add a review when staff change role or an engagement is extended. The useful result is a complete lifecycle: permission to begin, controlled access during the task and a verified end—not merely an installed remote desktop app.

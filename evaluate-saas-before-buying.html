Before You Buy SaaS: Test the Exit, Not Just the Demo

A practical buying checklist for task fit, permissions, billing and recoverable data.

By TechConsulting EditorialPublished 15 November 20264 min read

Practical guide · Examples are illustrative. Provider-described features are identified through official references, not a claim of hands-on product testing.

A smooth demonstration tells you how to start. A sample export helps you understand whether you can leave. Both deserve attention before a business moves its records into a new service.

This checklist is for an owner or team lead evaluating a subscription. It does not require you to perform a full migration during a trial. It asks for enough evidence to understand the operational commitment before paying.

Define what a successful purchase must do

Write one sentence describing the problem and one describing a successful result. For example, a hypothetical agency might need every new enquiry assigned to a named person with a visible next action. “We need a better CRM” is too broad to test.

Choose two or three realistic tasks. Define who should complete them and how you will judge success. Include the device, integrations and approximate record volume the team will use. A demo containing only a few clean records does not establish how a messy import will behave.

Check everyday access and permissions

Invite test users with different roles where the trial permits it. Confirm who can view client records, change settings, export information and remove users. Check what happens when an owner leaves or changes role.

Ask whether the required controls belong to the edition you can afford. Features in a product-wide security description may be unavailable on an entry plan. Record an unanswered question as unknown, not as a pass.

Inspect a representative export

Create sample records with the relationships, notes and files your work requires. Export them through the documented route. Open the output independently and check what is present, what is missing and whether another system could interpret it.

For example, HubSpot's record export documentation describes technical and association constraints. A record export should not be treated as proof that every part of an account is backed up. Apply the same question to any provider you evaluate.

Read the bill as well as the feature list

Separate regular price from introductory price. Ask about minimum seats, required setup, usage charges, connector fees and renewal. A monthly equivalent paid annually is not the same as a cancellable monthly subscription.

Budget internal implementation and administration time. List the people who must approve the purchase and the owner who will check the first invoice. The hidden-cost guide provides a more detailed quote worksheet.

Give support a realistic question

Ask a genuine product-fit question through the channel included in your proposed plan. Record how the answer resolves the issue and any limitation it reveals. One response cannot establish an ongoing service level, but it can identify a mismatch between the support offered and the support you need.

Do not upload confidential live data merely to make a trial feel realistic. Use approved sample data and follow your business's assessment process for sensitive information.

Record a decision you can explain

Mark each requirement pass, fail or unknown, with a source or test note. A failed essential requirement should not be outweighed by a long list of unused conveniences. An unknown essential requirement needs resolution before commitment.

Use this handoff: business problem; required tasks; qualifying plan; total-cost assumptions; permission findings; export findings; unresolved questions; accountable owner; decision and review date. That short record is more useful than a generic score out of ten.

If you choose to switch, continue with the migration checklist. If the decision is simply to end a service, use the cancellation checklist. Evaluation, moving data and ending access are related decisions, but they are not the same task.

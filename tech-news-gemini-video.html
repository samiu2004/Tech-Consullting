Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash makes video with sound, and costs less

Short video just got cheaper, and you can now edit it by talking to it.

By TechConsulting Editorial Published 5 November 2026 Sources checked 13 November 2026 2 min read

Short video just got cheaper, and you can now edit it by talking to it.

Google DeepMind published the model card for Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash on 5 November, and the model is generally available. It makes video, with audio, from text, images, audio, or video. Clips run up to 15 seconds instead of 10, and Google says pricing is 30 percent below the previous version. In plain words, you type or show it something, and you get a short video back, for less money than before.

The part that matters most for small teams is the editing. You tell it what to change and it changes it, rather than you dragging clips around a timeline at midnight. Google lists the Gemini app, YouTube, Google Flow, AI Studio, and Google Workspace among the places it is distributed, but it does not promise a date for your account, so check before you plan a campaign around it. Two details are worth knowing. Google says the videos carry SynthID watermarks, so AI content can be identified. And the model can change what people say in a video, but Google is restricting that for now.

Why it matters to you: a video workflow you shelved six months ago because it cost too much is worth another look. Short social clips are now cheaper to make, and the editing happens in plain words instead of specialist software.

What to do now

  • Try one short clip for social media, and price it against what a freelancer or a shoot would cost you.
  • Check every frame for wrong labels or odd hands before you use a clip.
  • Never edit a customer, a client, or a member of staff to say something they did not say. Not even as a joke.
  • Check that Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash is available in your account before you promise a video to a client.

SonicWall VPN flaw lets attackers skip multi-factor authentication

Your VPN asked for two proofs of identity. This flaw let an attacker skip the second one entirely.

By TechConsulting Editorial Published 6 November 2026 Sources checked 13 November 2026 2 min read

Your VPN asked for two proofs of identity. This flaw let an attacker skip the second one entirely.

A flaw in SonicWall firewalls, CVE-2026-19868, affects the SSL VPN service. Where the VPN uses SAML sign in, valid credentials for another login method can bypass the SAML controls and the multi-factor authentication they enforce. The second factor was not broken. It was simply never asked, because the attacker arrived by a different route. That is the pattern of this whole week: the strong front door held, and the attacker walked around it.

CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list on 6 November, with a federal deadline of 20 November. The Canadian Cyber Centre has confirmed exploitation. There is no workaround. The fix is SonicOS 7.3.4-7013 for Gen7 and 8.2.3-8015 for Gen8.

Why it matters to you: a VPN is the front door to a company network, and multi-factor authentication is the lock most teams trust. If an alternate login route skips that lock, the protection you think you have is not the protection you actually have. Stolen credentials are all an attacker needs to start.

What to do now

  • If you run a SonicWall VPN, patch now to SonicOS 7.3.4-7013 for Gen7 or 8.2.3-8015 for Gen8. Do not wait for the 20 November deadline.
  • Audit every alternate authentication route on your remote access systems, and switch off login methods you do not use.
  • Review who logged in, and how, since the flaw was disclosed, and rotate or review credentials that look wrong.
  • If you use a company VPN, ask your IT team whether your SonicWall is patched. It is a fair question, and it takes a minute to ask.

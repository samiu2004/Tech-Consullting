Iris Plaza breach may have exposed up to 7.7 million people

7.7 million people. That is the number at the centre of Japan's biggest shopping breach this month, and passwords were in the mix.

By TechConsulting Editorial Published 8 November 2026 Sources checked 13 November 2026 2 min read

7.7 million people. That is the number at the centre of Japan's biggest shopping breach this month, and passwords were in the mix.

Unauthorised access to an external cloud service used by the Iris Plaza store may have exposed up to 7.7 million people. That includes 5.8 million members and former members, including passwords, and 1.9 million non-members. The figure is a potential exposure under investigation, not a confirmed count of stolen records, and no misuse had been reported as of 5 November. Still, 7.7 million is a very big number, and passwords were in the mix.

Iris Plaza was not alone. In the same week, the Arara Message breach grew to about 77 million email addresses taken or possibly taken, and attackers used the service itself to send up to 7.13 million phishing emails. Arara was the fifth Japanese email delivery service breached in a single month. On 6 November, the Japanese government compiled an emergency countermeasures package, and the National Police Agency opened a nationwide joint investigation. When the same layer fails five times in a month, it becomes a property of the layer, not a run of bad luck.

Why it matters to you: a password that leaks in one breach is tried everywhere else. If you used the same password for Iris Plaza and another account, that other account is now at risk too, even though it was never breached.

What to do now

  • If you have an Iris Plaza account, change your password now, and do not reuse it anywhere else.
  • Change the same password on any other account where you used it.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication where it is offered, especially on email and shopping accounts.
  • Watch for phishing emails that use your shopping details, and do not click links in unexpected messages.

Source: ICHI Weekly Brief, 8 November 2026

Top story

France blamed Iran for the freezer attack that hit 700 fridges

10 November 2026France

Picture this. You walk into your supermarket one morning and the freezers are warm. That is what happened in France on the night of 11 and 12 October, when attackers hit 700 refrigeration systems. Supermarkets took the worst of it, along with a slaughterhouse, food processing plants, and hydro facilities. At least 15 fires broke out, and food spoiled. France has now blamed Iran. It says the cyber command of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps ordered the attack, and its Shahid Kaveh unit carried it out. Paris has asked for international arrest warrants for three people.

It did not stop there. A second wave hit supermarkets in Belgium, the Netherlands, Luxembourg, Sweden, Estonia, and Ireland overnight into 2 November. The attackers forced fridges into defrost mode, and shops had to throw the food away. That is a lot of good food in the bin. Then, on 5 November, five US agencies warned that attackers are going after industrial refrigeration and cold chain systems all over the world.

What it means: fridges and other industrial controllers should never be left open to the internet. If you run a shop or a food business, ask your supplier a simple question. Are your controllers updated, and are they kept offline?

Source: Le Monde and ICHI Weekly Brief, 8 November 2026

Cyber attacks and security

SonicWall / VPN security

SonicWall VPN flaw lets attackers skip multi-factor authentication

6 November 2026CVE-2026-19868

Here is a scary one for anyone who runs a company network. A flaw in SonicWall firewalls lets an attacker skip the normal sign in checks on the SSL VPN. Think of it like this. The VPN is supposed to ask for two proofs before it lets someone in, and this flaw lets an attacker walk past the second check. Where the VPN uses SAML sign in, valid credentials for another login method can bypass SAML controls and the multi-factor authentication they enforce. CISA added the flaw to its exploited list on 6 November, with a federal deadline of 20 November. There is no workaround. The fix is SonicOS 7.3.4-7013 for Gen7 and 8.2.3-8015 for Gen8.

What it means: if you run a SonicWall VPN, patch now and check which login methods are open. Do not wait for the deadline.

Source: ICHI Weekly Brief, 8 November 2026

WordPress / Website security

WordPress flaw lets attackers run code during updates

3 November 2026CVE-2026-119977

If your site runs on WordPress, listen up. A race in the way WordPress applies core updates lets an attacker with no login at all run code during the upgrade window. The attacker reaches a predictable staging folder while the site is updating. WordPress fixed the flaw on 28 October, and CISA added it to its catalogue on 3 November.

What it means: keep WordPress updated, and do not leave upgrades half done. Block public web access to the wp-content/upgrade folder.

Source: Taylor Baines

Amadeus / Travel bookings

Amadeus breach widens as Singapore Airlines reports 46,000 records affected

4 November 2026Singapore Airlines

If you flew recently, this one is worth a read. Singapore Airlines said about 46,000 booking records may have been affected through reservation provider Amadeus. It joins Cathay Pacific, Japan Airlines, ANA, Malaysia Airlines, and Asiana in the same incident. Amadeus says no payment data was taken and bookings were not modified. That is the good news. The awkward part is that booking details alone are enough for a convincing scam email.

What it means: booking details can be used for phishing. Treat unexpected emails about your booking with care.

Source: ICHI Weekly Brief, 8 November 2026

Iris Plaza / Online shopping

Iris Plaza breach may have exposed up to 7.7 million people

8 November 2026Iris Plaza

Here is a big one from Japan. Unauthorised access to an external cloud service may have exposed up to 7.7 million people. That includes 5.8 million members and former members, including passwords, and 1.9 million non-members. The figure is a potential exposure under investigation, and no misuse had been reported as of 5 November. Still, 7.7 million is a very big number, and passwords were in the mix.

What it means: if you have an Iris Plaza account, change your password and do not reuse it elsewhere.

Source: ICHI Weekly Brief, 8 November 2026

AI news

OpenAI / Images

ChatGPT Images 2.6 turns your pictures into GIFs, on every plan

4 November 2026OpenAI

Good news if you like making things with AI. OpenAI launched Images 2.6 on 4 November. It can turn your pictures into GIFs, and images generate 50% faster. The best part is the price. It is on every subscription tier, not only the expensive plan. So you can try it without upgrading.

What it means: a small business can now make a simple animated product shot without paying for a higher plan. Check the label at full size before you publish, because AI can misspell small print.

Source: Lilach Bullock

Google / Video

Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash makes video with sound, and costs less

5 November 2026Google DeepMind

Google is making video cheaper to create. Google DeepMind published the model card for Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash on 5 November. It makes video, with audio, from text, images, audio, or video. Clips run up to 15 seconds, and Google says pricing is 30% below the previous version. In plain words, you type or show it something, and you get a short video back, for less money than before.

What it means: short video is getting cheaper to make with AI. Check every frame for wrong labels or odd hands before you use a clip.

Source: BYOBot AI Daily Newsstand

Google DeepMind / Encryption

DeepMind found two weaknesses in old 1024-bit RSA encryption

6 November 2026Google DeepMind

Do not panic, but this one is fascinating. Google DeepMind said on 6 November that its AI agents, working with academic cryptographers, found two previously unknown weaknesses in 1024-bit RSA encryption. Both findings are theoretical rather than measured end to end. No production system relying on 1024-bit RSA was named, and DeepMind says modern encryption is untouched.

What it means: old 1024-bit keys should already be retired. This does not break the encryption used today, so there is no reason to panic.

Source: BYOBot AI Daily Newsstand

France blamed Iran for the freezer attack that hit 700 fridges

Your supermarket freezer is not supposed to be a target in a fight between countries. In France, it became one.

By TechConsulting Editorial Published 10 November 2026 Sources checked 13 November 2026 4 min read

Your supermarket freezer is not supposed to be a target in a fight between countries. In France, it became one.

On the night of 11 and 12 October, attackers hit 700 refrigeration systems in France. Supermarkets took the worst of it, along with a slaughterhouse, food processing plants, and hydro facilities. At least 15 fires broke out, food was damaged, and the attack had psychological impacts on the population. The French authorities described it as an unprecedented attack in terms of its scale and level of aggression.

On 9 November, France blamed Iran. It was the first time the French government had directly accused Tehran of a cyberattack, and it named those it holds responsible. France says the cyber command of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, known as IRGC-CEC, ordered the attack, and its Shahid Kaveh unit carried it out. The Paris prosecutor has requested international arrest warrants for three people. Hamid Reza Lashgarian, 65, heads IRGC-CEC and is a commander in the Al-Quds Force. He has been under US anti-terrorism sanctions since February 2024. Reza Salarvand, 35, is responsible for target selection and overseeing cyber intrusion campaigns. Mahdi Lashgarian, 37, is believed to play a leading role in Shahid Kaveh. Investigators pointed to a distinctive technical modus operandi, traces of Persian in the computer code, and IP addresses located in Iran. On 10 November, the Iranian ambassador in Paris was summoned.

This was not the first time the group had been noticed. It is also known as CyberAv3ngers, and it launched its first operations in November 2023. That month it took control of some equipment used in water management in the US and displayed a message of support for Gaza on device screens, without causing significant damage. Washington has offered a 10 million dollar reward for information about the group. It carried out attacks on refrigeration systems in Israel in March and April. The attack on French supermarkets is by far its most successful operation to date. The motive is still unclear. French cybersecurity firm Sekoia suggested one possible explanation: the recent deployment of French military assets to protect the Saudi oil terminal at Yanbu from attacks by pro-Iranian Houthi forces.

It did not stop with France. A second wave hit supermarkets in Belgium, the Netherlands, Luxembourg, Sweden, Estonia, and Ireland overnight into 2 November. The attackers forced fridges into defrost mode, and shops had to throw the food away. Danfoss said its controllers were involved, and France's CERT has also named Copeland and Dixell controllers. Then, on 5 November, five US agencies, CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA, and DOT, warned that attackers are going after industrial refrigeration and cold chain systems all over the world. There is one encouraging sign. Censys counted a 53 percent drop in internet-exposed Danfoss and Copeland controllers since the October waves. Taking controllers offline is working.

Why it matters to you: this attack shows that everyday equipment can become a target. A fridge controller left open to the internet is a door into a business, and when it is forced into heating mode, food spoils and fires can start. It affects shoppers, shop owners, and anyone who depends on cold food staying cold.

What to do now

  • If you run a shop or a food business, ask your supplier a simple question. Are your controllers updated, and are they kept offline?
  • Danfoss advises disconnecting AK-SC and AK-SM controllers from the internet. A factory reset alone does not stop repeat attacks.
  • Find every internet-connected refrigeration and building controller in your business, including ones managed by suppliers, and change any default passwords.
  • If you are a shopper and a freezer feels warm, tell the staff and do not buy food that has thawed.

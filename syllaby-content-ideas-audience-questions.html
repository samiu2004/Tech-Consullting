Don’t Know What to Post? Start With Audience Questions.

More brainstorming is not always the answer. Start with one question your audience is already asking.

Start with a question, not an empty calendar

You open your laptop, scroll through competitors and close it without choosing a topic. A notes app full of half-ideas does not necessarily make the next decision easier.

Instead of asking “What should I post?”, ask “What is one useful question I can answer for this audience?” That narrows the task. You are no longer trying to invent an entire content strategy before making a single post.

Research can help you find a starting point. It does not guarantee that every popular topic is relevant to the people you want to reach.

Look for questions you can genuinely answer

Review comments, enquiries, support conversations and recurring questions in relevant communities. Look for a specific uncertainty: what to choose, how to begin, what something costs or why a common attempt fails.

A hypothetical local bakery might notice repeated questions about storing bread, ordering ahead or choosing quantities for a small event. These are possible video briefs, not evidence of search demand or a campaign we have run.

Keep private customer details out of your content. Paraphrase a recurring question where appropriate rather than publishing a personal message without permission.

Use Syllaby to explore possible angles

Syllaby’s idea-discovery feature describes selecting a search engine or social platform and exploring topics by industry or category. It can help generate options before drafting a script.

Treat those options as leads, not proof. Check whether the question fits your audience, whether the underlying information is current and whether you can offer a useful answer beyond repeating other posts.

Do not describe a suggestion as a verified real-time trend unless you have checked the evidence behind that claim. Available sources, categories and features may change.

Turn the idea into a clear brief

Write down who the video is for, the question it answers and what the viewer should understand afterward. Add the evidence, demonstration or example you will use. Finish with an appropriate next step.

For example: “For first-time bread buyers, explain how to store a loaf overnight, using the bakery’s own product guidance and a clear demonstration.” That is more actionable than “make a video about bread.” This is an illustrative brief.

Use a suggested script to organize the answer, then edit it. Remove unsupported details, broad promises and advice that does not fit your situation. A strong hook should identify the real problem without exaggerating the outcome.

Build a small pipeline of useful ideas

Keep a short list of questions you are ready to answer, not an endless collection of vague topics. After publishing, notice which follow-up questions appear. Those can inform the next brief.

If you already have good ideas but cannot finish them, idea discovery may not be the bottleneck. The production workflow guide covers reducing handoffs; the batch guide explains how to group related work.

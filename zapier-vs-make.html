Zapier vs Make: compare the workflow before the bill

Understand Zapier tasks, Make credits and the maintenance questions that determine which automation tool fits your business.

By TechConsulting · Updated 18 November 2026 · Documentation-based

THE DECISION IN BRIEF

Map one real job before choosing your automation tool.

Zapier

Consider Zapier for an app-to-app workflow that your team can readily maintain.

Make

Consider Make when visual scenario design and data handling suit the person building it.

The short answer

Start with the task, not the brand. Write down what arrives, what should happen and who handles the exception. Then check whether Zapier or Make supports the exact app actions and permissions you need. Our recommendations are based on documentation; we have not benchmarked speed, reliability or ease of use.

Zapier describes automations as Zaps, with triggers and actions. Make calls its workflows scenarios and represents work through modules. Either can be a reasonable shortlist. The useful distinction is whether your chosen setup is understandable to the person who will maintain it six months from now.

What to compare

Question Zapier Make
What is the billing unit? Tasks for successful actions, subject to feature-specific rules Credits consumed by operations and certain AI usage
Does a unit mean the same thing? No: count the billable actions in your Zap No: inspect executed modules and applicable credit rules
What should you verify? Exact triggers, actions, plan gates and task allowance Modules, data handling, credit consumption and scheduling limits
Who must understand the setup? The person responsible for the Zap and failures The person responsible for the scenario and failures

Why headline prices can mislead

Zapier lists Professional starting from $19.99 per month on its pricing page. The selected task allowance and billing option determine the payable total; confirm both at checkout. Make’s current pricing page explains credits, but we could not establish a stable plan price from its accessible listing, so we are not publishing a guessed figure.

A Zapier task and a Make credit are not interchangeable. Zapier says a successfully completed action counts as a task, while polling for new data does not. Make documents one credit per operation for non-AI apps; AI features can introduce other consumption rules and provider costs.

Imagine 300 eligible enquiries in a month. If each enquiry produces exactly three billable Zapier actions, that portion of the workflow uses 900 tasks. This is a simplified example, not a measured bill. Extra actions, replays and different feature rules can change it. Estimate Make separately from the modules that actually execute; do not relabel those 900 tasks as 900 credits.

A workflow worth piloting

Use a sample enquiry form, a test customer list and an internal notification channel. Have the automation create or update the customer record and alert the owner. Keep outbound customer messages disabled until your checks pass.

Test a duplicate enquiry, a missing email address and an expired connection. Ask what happens after a timeout: will a retry create a second customer or send another message? Check execution history and who receives the failure alert.

Measure the work required to diagnose the failure, not just the time saved when everything goes well. Keep a short handover note listing the owner, connected accounts and recovery steps. These are proposed pilot checks, not results from a test we have run.

The costs that sit outside the subscription

Allow for separate app subscriptions, AI-provider charges, setup time and maintenance. If client information passes through the workflow, review permissions and data handling before connecting production accounts. A working integration still needs an accountable owner.

If your CRM already provides the complete automation, try that native feature first. Adding another platform may create a bill and another failure point without removing enough work.

Our decision

Pick the tool that completes your specific workflow within budget and that your team can repair. Shortlist Zapier for its trigger-and-action approach; shortlist Make if its scenario model fits your builder’s needs. Validate exact integrations and a representative month’s usage before buying an annual plan.
